It is 9:12 at night, the river is climbing over the road above you, and the only exit left in this car park is the one door everyone told you never to open.
Three hours until the flood crests. Your phone buzzes on the dashboard. "Marlow Street, leave the car park now." You are on level B3, the lowest floor, and the shutter at the top of the ramp has just started to close.
You run for the ramp. Water is already sliding down it, thin and shiny, like a tongue licking the concrete. Battery at eighty-two percent. Water at your ankles.
A delivery rider skids down the slope pushing a loaded bike. This is Jojo. He has a helmet with ears, a yellow bag, and a small tool pouch clipped on his belt. "Shutter's stuck halfway," he says. "Nobody's driving out of here."
Behind a pillar, an old man in a green vest waves a torch. Mr. Bell, the night attendant, sixty years on his feet and still smiling. Beside him, Mrs. Alder, round and unbothered, guarding a shopping trolley full of tinned peaches.
Mr. Bell points the torch along the far wall. "Lift still works. Fire stairs behind it. And that door at the end of level B2, marked do not use, goes nowhere. Don't touch it."
Jojo grins at the sign. "Everything in this place says do not use."
Then the lights flicker, the lift display blinks, and the lift pings up to level B1 all by itself.
Water at your knees on the ramp, three hours left, and you have to choose how to climb.
Freeze. Option one, run up the car ramp before the water gets deeper. Option two, take the lift, because it is fast and dry. Option three, climb the fire stairs behind the lift.
Pause the video. Decide now. Your clock keeps running.
You choose option three. Jojo hauls his bike onto his shoulder, Mrs. Alder pushes her trolley up the first steps, and the three of you follow Mr. Bell's torch into the stairwell.
Behind you, the lift sinks back down with a tired little chime, and its lights go out.
The pit at the bottom of a lift shaft is the first place in a building to flood. The moment water touches the motor, the lift stops, with passengers inside. And that ramp? Moving water only fifteen centimetres deep can knock a grown adult off their feet. Level ground is a river's favourite road.
Stairs are boring. Stairs are why you are alive.
On level B2 the stairwell door opens onto a wide grey floor and the sound of rushing water from somewhere below. Seventy-one percent battery. Two hours forty left.
Jojo checks his phone and winces. "No signal down here."
Mr. Bell sets his torch on a pillar. "Street door is at the top of this stairwell. Chained from outside since the last storm. I never got the key back."
Mrs. Alder pats her trolley. "Then we wait with peaches."
Nobody laughs. The water on B2 is still dry, but you can hear it knocking on the walls like a patient visitor.
You walk along the wall to look for a vent, a window, anything. And there it is. A tall grey door at the end of the corridor, with an old red sign. Exit D. Do not use. Loading bay.
Jojo whispers. "That one?"
Mr. Bell frowns. "It jammed years ago. Hinges are rusty. I never even touched the handle."
Your phone shows no signal at all. Battery at sixty-four percent. One bar flickers, appears, disappears.
Freeze. Option one, hold the phone up near the vent and send a single short message with your level and street. Option two, call the emergency line and keep talking as long as you can. Option three, switch the phone off completely to save every percent for later.
Pause the video. Decide now. One bar will not last.
You choose option one. You type four lines. "Marlow Street car park. Four people. Level B2. Water rising." Send.
The message climbs out of the dark and leaves. One word, delivered.
A call needs a steady signal for the whole minute, and one bar cannot hold it. A message needs only a second. Rescuers read messages while they are driving. And if you switch the phone off, there will be no answer when somebody finally writes back.
You hold the phone close to your chest and it warms your hand. Sixty-one percent.
Mrs. Alder squeezes your hand. "If you'd like us to keep telling you stories, subscribe, and we will meet you in the next one."
Jojo grins. "Oh, I heard that."
Then the first real water arrives. Not a trickle, a lip of brown, rolling under the stairwell door and spreading across the floor like spilled tea. Water at your ankles on B2.
Mr. Bell looks down at the water and then up at the stairs. "At this speed, B2 is under in an hour."
You glance at the stair door at the top. Chained. You look back at Exit D. Rusty. Mrs. Alder watches your face and quietly says, "When you've looked at both, you already know."
Jojo kneels and opens his small tool pouch. "Let me try something."
Freeze. Option one, wait for rescue on the stairs, a floor above the water. Option two, shout and bang on the chained street door until somebody hears. Option three, go back to Exit D and work out why it is stuck.
Pause the video. Decide now. The water does not wait.
You choose option three. Jojo presses his ear against the cold metal. He tries the handle. Nothing. He wipes his hands on his coat, looks at the frame, and mutters, "It isn't locked. It is swollen."
Rain, rust and a decade of damp. He picks a flat tool from his pouch, slides it into the gap, and the whole door gives a faint creak, like an old man stretching.
The street door was chained because someone wanted it shut. Exit D was marked because someone wanted it left alone. Neither sign said it was unsafe. One said do not enter. One said do not use. And the old loading bay, you remember, always sat higher than the street.
It is half past ten. Fifty-two percent. Water at your knees.
Then the stairwell lights die. Everything goes black, except the little red glow of Mr. Bell's torch. A deep metallic groan rolls through the walls. The building is talking.
Water at your waist. Forty-one percent. One hour left.
Freeze. Option one, push on the door together with all your weight. Option two, take the hinge pins out with Jojo's tool and lift the door off its frame. Option three, run for the chained street door and try to break the chain.
Pause the video. Decide now. The door is your only chance.
You choose option two. Jojo kneels, taps the first hinge pin, then the second, then the third, one by one with the heel of his tool. A bolt clicks, a pin slides out, and the door leans toward you with a long, tired sigh.
All four of you lift it together. The door tips outward, falls flat across the doorway, and a cold, clean wind blows straight into your face.
You were not pushing at a lock. You were pushing against a hinge that rust had welded in place. A swollen door fights back harder the more you shove it. Take out the pins, and it forgets how to resist.
Beyond the door is a concrete ramp, dry and steep, climbing out of the dark toward a pale orange sky. The loading bay. Two storeys above the street.
Mr. Bell steps through first, then Mrs. Alder with her trolley, then Jojo with his bike. Water at your hips behind you. Thirty-seven percent.
You climb to the top, and the street below is a brown river with streetlights standing in it like candles in a birthday cake. A rescue boat rolls past. A rescue crew waves a torch at the four of you.
At midnight the flood crests, two metres above the ramp you first walked down. Mrs. Alder shares peaches with a rescuer and says, "I told you, I always bring supplies."
Mr. Bell reads the sign on the loading bay door one last time. "I wrote that sign myself," he says. "Thirty years ago. It means the door is old, not that it is dangerous."
So be honest. At the foot of that ramp, with water at your knees, would you have taken the stairs?
The door marked do not use was the only way out that was ever open.
